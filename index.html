Conveyancing Services Brisbane Checklist Explained

The clearest first step is to describe the deal in plain terms: purchase, sale, title transfer, off-the-plan contract, auction purchase or commercial property. That framing changes what must be checked, because each pathway has a different pressure point before the buyer becomes bound. It also helps a solicitor or conveyancing practitioner quote for the actual work rather than a generic file.

  • Purchase: contract terms, finance dates, searches, duty and settlement timing.
  • Sale: contract preparation, Form 2 seller disclosure and release of funds direction.
  • Title transfer: parties, consideration, existing mortgage position and registration steps.
  • Off-the-plan: sunset clauses, disclosure documents and building progress risk.
  • Auction: review before bidding, since the usual cooling-off protection does not apply.
  • Commercial property: leases, outgoings, GST treatment and special conditions.

For a first purchase, grants and concessions matter as much as legal timing. A seller needs confidence that mandatory disclosure is complete. An auction buyer needs answers before the hammer falls, which is why it helps to read common conveyancing mistakes in Brisbane before settling on a strategy.

Put contract review before calendar pressure builds

Review belongs before dates become hard to move. For buyers, the useful output is an action list: conditions to satisfy, searches to order, duty to arrange and documents the lender will expect. For sellers, the output is a contract and disclosure position that can be issued without hesitation.

Ask for the review to identify three things. First, which clauses create personal risk, such as finance, building and pest, deposit release or sunset timing. Second, which searches are standard for the property and which are optional because of its location or history. Third, who will chase special conditions if the other side, the lender or a body corporate responds slowly.

  • Before acceptance or bidding: confirm whether terms should be varied while leverage remains.
  • After signing: lock in critical dates, search sequence, duty arrangements and lender contact.
  • Before settlement: check electronic settlement authority, funds direction and transfer documents.

This order matters because settlement depends on more than one party being ready at the same moment.

Separate the professional fee from outside charges

A low headline fee is not the whole settlement cost. The practical comparison separates the practitioner's professional fee from disbursements: title and property searches, registration charges, settlement-related items and transfer duty. The routine professional fee can be confirmed in writing, while government and third-party amounts should be itemised so the total is visible before commitment.

That separation lets a buyer compare like with like. It also gives a seller a clearer view of deductions before the net proceeds are estimated. When reviewing a quote, look for the assumptions behind the fixed amount. If the matter turns out to be off-the-plan, an auction file, a family transfer or commercial property, ask whether the quoted scope still fits.

  • Professional fee: the work of review, advice, negotiation support and settlement management.
  • Disbursements: searches and charges paid to outside parties for information or registration.
  • Duty: transfer duty calculated on the dutiable value and arranged before lodgement deadlines bite.

Written clarity is not cosmetic. It reduces the chance that a necessary search or registration step is discovered late.

Time the risk around exchange, auction and settlement

Queensland timing changes the order of advice. For most residential contracts signed privately, the cooling-off period runs for five business days after the buyer receives the contract signed by both parties. Ending the contract in that window can bring a termination penalty of up to 0.25 per cent of the purchase price. Auction property is different: the cooling-off period does not apply, so the safer path is review before bidding.

The real question is whether any issue must be resolved before the buyer is bound. A finance clause may protect a private treaty buyer, but only while the date and lender process line up. A building concern may justify a search or inspection before conditions fall due. For settlement, electronic lodgement through PEXA means documents, funds and instructions need to align across buyer, seller, lender and practitioner.

  • Private treaty: use any short post-signing window for urgent questions, not for starting review from scratch.
  • Auction: treat pre-bidding advice as essential rather than optional.
  • Off-the-plan: check disclosure and sunset risk while termination rights, if any, still exist.

When timing feels tight, ask which deadline can move and which cannot. That single answer often reveals the priority order.

Build a settlement file that survives contact delays

The final stage rewards preparation. Ownership under the Torrens system depends on correct transfer and registration, so the file should show who signs, who pays, who releases funds and who attends to lodgement. If finance is involved, the bank's document and settlement requirements can set the pace as firmly as the contract date.

A resilient file usually holds the signed contract, special conditions, search results, duty instructions, mortgagee details, identification steps for electronic settlement and the funds direction. Keep version names plain so the latest authority is easy to recognise. Before the day, confirm the amount required, any shortfall plan, and the point at which keys or possession change hands.

The aim is not to remove every variable. It is to make each variable visible early enough for a calm choice. A checklist works best when it records the decision, the owner of the next step and the date by which that step must close.

  1. Define the transaction. Write whether the matter is a purchase, sale, transfer, off-the-plan contract, auction purchase or commercial property, including any lender, tenants or family parties.
  2. Request an itemised scope. Ask for the professional fee in writing and have searches, registration items, settlement charges and transfer duty shown separately.
  3. Map the binding dates. List contract review, finance, building and pest, cooling-off or auction bidding, duty and settlement so each date has an owner.
  4. Run the review sequence. Before signing or bidding, resolve risky clauses. After signing, order searches and duty steps. Before settlement, confirm electronic authority, funds direction and transfer documents.
  5. Close the file with evidence. Keep the latest signed contract, search results, duty instructions, lender requirements and settlement authority together for a clean handover.

Common questions

What should be included in a conveyancing quote for routine Brisbane work? Expect the professional fee to be confirmed in writing and the outside amounts to be listed separately. Those outside amounts can include title and property searches, registration charges and transfer duty. If the matter is off-the-plan, commercial, an auction purchase or a family transfer, ask whether the routine quote still matches the work required.

Does cooling-off apply if I buy at auction in Queensland? No. The described Queensland rule excludes property bought at auction, so the practical protection is review before bidding rather than review after the hammer falls. For private treaty purchases, most residential contracts carry a five business day cooling-off period starting when the buyer receives the contract signed by both parties, and ending during that period may cost up to 0.25 per cent of the price.

When should a lender be involved in the conveyancing plan? Early, if finance is part of the purchase. Settlement depends on the bank being ready with documents and funds, not only on the buyer and seller agreeing dates. Ask what the lender requires, how instructions are given for electronic settlement and which party will chase outstanding items before the settlement workspace is ready.
